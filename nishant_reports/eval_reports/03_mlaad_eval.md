## Dataset

**Fake audio source:** [mueller91/MLAAD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mueller91/MLAAD) on HuggingFace (gated, CC-BY-NC 4.0)

MLAAD is a multilingual anti-spoofing dataset containing synthetic speech generated by 140+ TTS models across 51 languages. We sampled 100 English fake files from 10 diverse TTS engines (10 files each). Audio is WAV format.

**Real audio source:** ASVspoof5 eval set bonafide files (same 100 files used in Eval 02). These are crowdsourced real speech recordings in FLAC format.

## Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Accuracy** | **44.5%** |
| **Precision** | 35.1% |
| **Recall** | 13.0% |
| **F1-Score** | 19.0% |
| **EER** | 62.0% |

At production threshold (0.55): 12/100 fakes detected, 19/100 reals falsely flagged.

Score distributions:
- **Real:** mean 0.377, median 0.336
- **Fake:** mean 0.289, median 0.252

The model scores fake audio *lower* than real audio — the opposite of what a working detector should do.

## Takeaways

The ML engine fails on modern TTS engines. The model was trained on older attack types (ASVspoof 2021 LA era) and does not generalize to current commercial or open-source TTS systems. ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Gemini, and other state-of-the-art engines produce audio that the model confidently classifies as real.

This is the most critical finding across all three evaluations: the model needs to be retrained on modern TTS output to be useful in production against current threats.
